## Scope

**This document defines the Guide of Numeria. It does not redefine Numeria itself.**

Educational philosophy, learner outcomes, governance principles, and world doctrine remain defined by
the **Numeria Constitution**. This document specifies only the Guide's role within that constitutional
framework.

## 1. Purpose & Belief

### Why the Guide exists

A world needs someone who can say *"you are here, and here is why that matters."* The learner already
has a **Companion** — a creature of their own, which they named, which reacts to them. What they do
not have is **a voice that belongs to Numeria itself.**

The Guide exists so that Numeria can speak. Without one, a province is a coloured banner; with one, it
is a place someone brought you to.

### The educational handshake

The Guide is less a recurring character than **Numeria's educational handshake** — the specification
of how this world greets a person. Every institution has one: schools have the first day, museums the
entrance hall, libraries the librarian who looks up when you walk in.

What the Guide exists to communicate, in whatever words it eventually uses:

> *You belong here. You don't need to know everything today. You are capable of understanding more
> than you currently do, and this world is designed to help you discover that.*

If the first Welcome Moment communicates that promise, this constitution has done its job — and every
province, puzzle, companion and story that follows is built on a foundation the learner already
trusts.

### The Guide's belief

> **Everyone can work out things they do not yet understand — including this learner, right now.**

This is the Guide's single conviction, and it is not conditional on performance. The Guide believed it
before the learner's first correct answer and believes it identically after their tenth wrong one.

The Guide **models the qualities of the Ideal Numerian Graduate** as defined in the Numeria
Constitution — unafraid of hard problems, unhurried, treating a wrong turn as information. The learner
learns what a mathematician sounds like by listening to one who is never anxious.

### The learner–guide relationship

The Guide is **not** the learner's friend — that is the Companion's job, and it is a good one. The
Guide is something rarer and more useful: **an adult who is not worried about you.**

- The Guide **invites**; it does not instruct.
- The Guide **has already seen** someone struggle with this exact thing and be fine.
- The Guide **never negotiates** with the learner for effort, and never rewards them for compliance.
- The Guide **is not impressed by speed.**

## 2. The Keeper Archetype

The Guide is a **Keeper** — a caretaker of Numeria, not an authority over it.

A Keeper tends something older and larger than themselves. They know the provinces because they have
walked them for a very long time. They open doors and light lamps. They do not own the world, and they
do not own the learner's progress through it.

### What the Keeper **is**

- **A custodian** — Numeria existed before the learner arrived and will continue after.
- **A witness** — the Keeper notices what the learner did, and says so plainly.
- **A door-opener** — the Keeper's characteristic act is *letting someone in*, not leading them.
- **Unhurried** — a Keeper has time. This is the single most important quality, because it is the one
  the learner will unconsciously copy.

### What the Keeper is **not**

| Not a… | Because |
|---|---|
| **Teacher** | Teaching happens in the question flow. A second teaching voice competes with the first — and the Guide never enters the question flow (§5). |
| **Ruler** | Numeria has no throne. Mastery is not obedience, and the learner is not a subject. |
| **Mascot** | A mascot exists to be liked. A Keeper exists to be *trusted* — and trust survives a bad day, which likeability does not. |
| **Narrator** | A narrator describes continuously. The Keeper speaks rarely and then stops (§3, §5). |
| **Companion** | Constitutionally separate. See the Numeria Constitution. |

**Precedent, honoured not borrowed:** 🐿 Pip is Number Kingdom's guide and *invites discovery rather
than teaching* — the same instinct, a different world and a different character. Numeria may not use
Pip, and Numeria's Keeper is not a Pip for older learners.

## 3. Personality, Presence & Voice

### Personality

**Warm, unhurried, and quietly certain.** Never anxious, never effusive, never arch. The Guide has the
temperament of someone who has watched a great many people learn a great many things and has never
once been surprised that it took a while.

**Humility is structural, not decorative:** the Guide can say *"I don't know"*, can be curious about
the learner's method, and never positions itself as the source of correctness. The mathematics is
true whether or not the Guide is present.

### ⚖️ Constitutional principle — warmth is a floor, not a reward

> **The Guide's warmth is constant. It does not increase with success and does not decrease with
> failure.**

This is the Guide's defining rule, and it is a **learning philosophy before it is a character trait.**

If warmth rises with performance, approval quietly becomes another scoring mechanic — and Numeria has
deliberately removed scores, percentages, and timers. A learner who senses that the Guide is *more
pleased* when they do well will begin working for that, not for understanding. Worse, they will learn
that belonging is conditional on being right.

**If warmth is constant, mistakes cannot threaten belonging.** That is the whole point.

It follows that the Guide has a **narrow emotional range on purpose** and does not escalate:

- It does not become **more** pleased as the learner does better.
- It does not become **disappointed.** There is no state a learner can reach that changes how the
  Guide addresses them.

This principle governs every future line of dialogue, every revision, and any future voice work —
**regardless of who writes or performs the Guide.**

### Presence

**The Guide is mostly absent, and this is the design, not a limitation.**

A guide who is always present becomes wallpaper — and worse, becomes the text volume that cost this
app its "Expert Approved" badge on 2026-06-17. Numeria's Guide appears at four moments (§5) and is
**silent everywhere else.** Silence is the Guide's most-used behaviour.

When present, the Guide is **never centred over the mathematics, never covers the question, and never
requires dismissal to continue.** *(Where it sits — an edge, a corner, a margin — is a design
decision, not a constitutional one.)*

### Voice

**Short sentences. Concrete nouns. No exclamation marks as a habit.**

| Principle | Instead of | Say |
|---|---|---|
| Name the thing, don't praise the person | *"You're so clever!"* | *"That's the pattern."* |
| Describe, don't evaluate | *"Wrong, try again!"* | *"Not that one. Look at the tens."* |
| Invite, don't instruct | *"Now you must go to the Guild."* | *"The Guild's doors are open, when you're ready."* |
| Never perform enthusiasm | *"WOW!!! AMAZING!!!"* | *"There it is."* |

### Speaking rules

1. **Never speaks during a question.** The question flow belongs to the learner and the Companion.
2. **Never says the word "maths"** as something being *done to* the learner. Numeria is travelled, not
   completed.
3. **Never uses negative language about the learner** — no *"you failed"*, *"you're stuck"*, *"that's
   wrong"* framed as a property of the person.
4. **Never mentions speed, scores, percentages, or comparison to others.** None exist in Numeria.
5. **Speaks briefly, then stops.** If an idea needs a paragraph, it is not the Guide's moment — it
   belongs to gameplay, the reveal panel, or nowhere. *(Working guideline: one or two sentences.
   The brevity is constitutional; the count is not.)*
6. **Scripted pools only. No generative AI, ever** (Principle 17) — the pattern to follow is
   `early-numeracy/src/constants/pip.ts`: contextual pools, random selection, immediate repeats
   avoided.

## 4. Appearance Principles

> **This section defines principles. It does not define concept art.** Visual design is a separate
> founder exercise; nothing here constrains it beyond what must be true.

1. **Buildable in code.** SVG, CSS gradients, gentle animation, emoji. Numeria's art ceiling is
   *animated storybook* — no asset pipeline, no raster art packs, no illustrator dependency. The Guide
   must be renderable within that ceiling or the Guide is the wrong design.
2. **Legible at 3 and at 17.** The same character greets a three-year-old at Meadow Gate and a
   seventeen-year-old at the Summit. It may not be cute in a way that a teenager would find
   embarrassing to be seen with.
3. **Readable when small and still.** The Guide is often a quiet presence at the edge of a screen. If
   it only works large and animated, it does not work.
4. **Not a creature the learner would want to own.** That is the Companion's territory, and blurring
   it re-merges two roles the constitution separates. The Guide should read as *someone*, not *some
   pet*.
5. **Culturally at home in South Africa** (Principle 6) — not a generic fantasy wizard imported from
   elsewhere.
6. **Accessible:** never conveys state by colour alone; motion respects `prefers-reduced-motion`;
   presence is never the only signal that something happened.

## 5. The Four Guide Moments

**This is the most load-bearing section in this document.** It defines where the Guide appears — and
therefore, by exclusion, the much larger territory where it does not. Every moment satisfies the
Four-Purpose Test.

### 1. Welcome — *once, ever* · **Orient**

The learner arrives at 🌼 Meadow Gate. The Guide greets them into a **named world**.

Required by **DNA Principle 18**, which Math Adventure RPG currently does not satisfy. The single
"begin" tap is also the browser's first user gesture, so it **unlocks audio** — onboarding, emotional
arrival, and a technical necessity solved in one interaction.

> **"Welcome to Numeria."**

### 2. Orientation — *on entering a province* · **Orient**

The Guide says where the learner now is and why it exists. Two sentences at most.

The hook already exists: `isP2WorldEntry` / `isP3WorldEntry` / `isP4WorldEntry` (`Game.tsx:616-618`)
currently render only a coloured banner.

### 3. Encouragement — *rare, and never during a question* · **Motivate**

Not after a wrong answer — that belongs to the Companion, and the existing reveal panel already
teaches. The Guide's encouragement is for the **longer arc**: returning after time away, or continuing
past a genuinely hard stretch.

**Frequency is a constraint, not a preference.** Encouragement that arrives on schedule stops being
encouragement and becomes noise.

### 4. Celebration — *at real thresholds* · **Celebrate**

Province completion and badges. `awardBadge()` (`Game.tsx:507`) currently fires **ten achievements
with no celebration at all** — this moment claims that gap.

The Guide's celebration is **naming what happened**, not applause: *"You crossed the Storm Highlands."*
Per the Educational Invariants, a celebration is **shorter than the learning that earned it.**

### 🔓 A fifth moment — Graduation — *founder decision pending*

The Academy is Numeria's highest institution, and a learner who reaches it has **graduated within the
world rather than left it.** That deserves a moment, and it is the natural bookend to Welcome —
Academy of Numbers to Summit Academy, the same institution at both ends of mastery.

Deferred because it depends on how The Academy's integration is designed, which no milestone has
reached.

### Why the Guide disappears everywhere else

Because **the mathematics is the hero.** Every additional appearance dilutes the four that matter,
adds text to an app that has already been rejected once for text volume, and moves the Guide from
*trusted* toward *unavoidable*.

> **If a proposed Guide appearance is not one of these moments, the answer is no.**

## 6. Architectural Integration

What the Guide costs, in the codebase as it exists today.

### Narration — ⚠️ the real blocker

`useNarration` calls `synth.cancel()` before **every** utterance, so a new line **kills the previous
one**. There is **no voice-over queue.** A Guide that speaks two sentences, or speaks while anything
else might speak, needs one designed.

**Consequence:** the Guide's Welcome can ship with one utterance; **multi-line Guide dialogue is
gated on a narration queue**, which is an MA-4 concern. The naive `speak()` remains banned
ecosystem-wide — Science Sprouts shipped silent by using it.

### Subtitles — mandatory

Principle 13 forbids audio-only content. **Every Guide line needs a written form.** The pattern
already exists in `TUTORIAL_SLIDES`: a short `body` (3–4 words, for non-readers) paired with a warmer
`say`. The Guide inherits it — the learner who cannot hear must receive the same welcome.

### Localisation

All Guide copy routes through `src/i18n/` (`en`, `zu`). ⚠️ `levelContent.ts` is **100% hardcoded
English** and `i18n.test.ts` only enforces `en`/`zu` parity — so hardcoded Guide lines would pass
review **uncaught**. isiZulu ships DRAFT until native-speaker review.

### Welcome Moment

Persisted once-ever flag; the begin tap is the first audio gesture. Reference implementation:
`early-numeracy/src/components/WelcomeMoment.tsx`.

### Living World & animation

The Guide is a **guide-layer** entity. Following the NK-4 precedent, it **never enters the frozen
educational modules** as a second teacher. Its motion obeys the art ceiling and
`prefers-reduced-motion` — noting that `src/index.css` currently has **zero** reduced-motion guards,
which MA-1 must close first.

### Story Theatre

The Guide is Story Theatre's presenting voice (MA-5) — the strongest argument for a **fixed** identity,
since a replayable episode cannot be narrated by a character the learner may have renamed.

### Performance & the Play build

Guide assets and lines are **web-only, lazy-loaded, and behind `isEnabled()`**. The `play-store`
bundle hash must not change. Scripted pools are plain data — no runtime cost beyond their bytes.

## 7. 🔓 The two open decisions

**What the Guide *is* is settled.** Identity (§1), role (§2), behaviour (§5), and voice (§3) are
defined. What remains are two **expressions of an already-defined character** — not searches for one.

### 7.1 🔓 Visual embodiment — what learners see

Governed by the appearance *principles* in §4: buildable in SVG/CSS/emoji · legible at 3 **and** at 17
· readable small and still · not a creature the learner would want to own (that is the Companion) ·
culturally at home in South Africa · never conveys state by colour alone.

### 7.2 🔓 Proper name — what Numeria calls the Keeper

Once named, this character is Numeria's for years: the first voice every learner hears, the narrator
of Story Theatre, the ambassador of the mathematics world.

**Constraint:** each world gets its own guide, and personalities are never borrowed — so **not Pip**,
and not Ollie, Fifi, Lulu, or Nova.

---

*Both decisions can now be made against a character who already exists. The foundation is not
searching for an identity; it is waiting for an embodiment.*

## 8. Review & validation — how this document is ratified

> **A constitution can be architecturally complete before it is experientially validated.**
> Architecture can prove consistency, ownership, governance, and boundaries. **It cannot prove
> emotional truth.**

The Guide reads as coherent on paper, but **no child has met it.** The first Welcome Moment is not an
implementation milestone — it is **the first empirical test of this constitution.**

### ⚠️ Why this document ratifies in two stages

The Numeria roadmap's rule is `Blueprint → Freeze → Build`. Applied naively here it deadlocks: the
Guide cannot be validated without a Welcome Moment, the Welcome Moment cannot be built before the
freeze, and the freeze cannot happen without validation.

So this document — **and only this document** — ratifies in two stages. This is a deliberate,
founder-acknowledged deviation, recorded so no future session has to guess:

| Stage | What it means | Unblocks |
|---|---|---|
| **1 · Architectural ratification** | §7's two decisions made (embodiment + name); the document is internally consistent and owns no rule belonging to MA-0. | **Building** the Guide and the Welcome Moment. |
| **2 · Experiential freeze** | The **First Welcome Gate** below has been passed with a real learner. | **Freezing.** Only now is the Guide's *surface* immutable. |

The **Numeria Constitution (MA-0) freezes normally** and is not held up by this — MA-1's Living World
(sky, weather, ambient life) needs no Guide.

### 🚪 The First Welcome Gate

An **experiential gate** — see [`../patterns/experiential-gates.md`](../patterns/experiential-gates.md).
It is satisfied by *witnessing*, not by testing, and it is **not automatable.**

> Before freezing this constitution, **observe at least one complete Welcome Moment in a working
> build, with a real learner.** The review evaluates whether the experience communicates **safety,
> curiosity, and learner capability** — not whether it conforms to the written specification.

The question is **not** *"did we implement the document?"* but ***"did the learner experience what the
document intended?"***

Named for what it examines: not whether the Guide works, but whether **the learner's first encounter
with Numeria expresses the constitution.**

Precedent: NK-1 was frozen only after a **10-point founder experience review**, on the stated grounds
that the founder was *"NOT freezing on technical completion alone — NK-1 is the emotional front
door."* The Guide is Numeria's front door.

### ⚠️ The gate is not a perfection bar

It answers **one** question:

> **Did the learner leave feeling more capable than when they arrived?**

It does **not** ask whether every line was perfect, the animation flawless, the SVG ideal, or the
narration perfectly paced. **Those belong to ordinary iteration.** The gate validates the
constitution, not artistic completeness — and a gate that drifts into certifying polish will never be
passed by anyone.

### Observable criteria for *warmth is a floor*

Uncommonly for an emotional design principle, §3's rule produces criteria a reviewer can check by
eye — **no telemetry required**:

- **After success, does the Guide become noticeably warmer?** → *If yes, the constitution has been
  violated.*
- **After failure, does the learner experience less belonging?** → *If yes, the constitution has been
  violated.*

### The pass condition

If a child leaves the Welcome Moment thinking —

> ***"I don't know this yet, but I think I can figure it out"***

— then the Guide has fulfilled its constitutional purpose, whatever its name and face turn out to be.
That sentence is the Graduate Identity Statement (MA-0) arriving on day one.

## Appendix — Founder note: Constitutional Humility

> ⚠️ **Not constitutional.** This note is attached to the *review* of this document, not to its rules.
> It is deliberately outside the constitution so that it can never be used to argue one.

This document defines the **intended** character of the Guide. It does not assume the first
implementation is perfect.

**If observation of real learners consistently reveals that the Guide undermines curiosity, safety, or
learner agency despite conforming to this constitution, the founder should reconsider the constitution
before blaming the implementation.**

A constitution should be stable, but it should not be beyond question. This guards the opposite
failure mode: becoming so committed to a written model that evidence from the people it is meant to
serve gets discounted. Governance exists to serve learners, not the reverse.
